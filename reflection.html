Where the degree leaves me.

01 SELF-INTRODUCTION

A Computer Engineering graduate who verifies before he trusts.

I am a Computer Engineering graduate from Asia Pacific University (CGPA 3.43/4.0), and this E-Portfolio is my reflection on what four years of study, one professional internship, and eleven independently earned certifications have actually made of me as an engineer.

Professionalism, for me, was not something a lecture defined — it was something I learned by doing IT Internal Audit work at Protiviti Middle East. Testing Identity and Access Management controls, reconciling SIEM asset records against reality, and drafting findings that a client would actually read taught me that an engineer's job does not end when a system works; it ends when you can prove it works, to someone who was not in the room while you built it. That experience, carried out across teams in Malaysia, Saudi Arabia and Egypt, also gave me a working sense of how standards, expectations and communication styles shift across cultures and jurisdictions, and why that has to be planned for rather than assumed.

My exposure to emerging technology has been hands-on rather than theoretical: building a locally-hosted LLM pipeline for MeetNote so that meeting audio never has to leave a user's device, integrating an ESP32 sensor glove into PulseSense's real-time patient monitoring view, and designing the UI/UX for Genie, an AI campus assistant now used as a research project at APU. Eleven certifications — spanning networking, cloud, cybersecurity and applied AI — sit underneath that work, almost all of them pursued outside formal coursework because I wanted the fundamentals to be mine, not borrowed for one assignment.

That habit is what I mean by independent, life-long learning: moving from campus IT support, to hardware engineering assistance, to AI software development, to formal audit practice in under two years, each time picking up the tools a new context demanded rather than waiting to be taught them. I do not think that habit is finished — it is the one part of this portfolio I expect to keep re-writing for the rest of my career.

02 SWOT ANALYSIS

An honest read on where I stand.

Every point below is tied to something that actually happened during the degree — a specific project, internship engagement, or certification — not a general impression of myself.

S

Strengths

  • Rare combination of domains: IT audit/compliance (Protiviti), applied AI (Whisper, Ollama), cloud (AWS, Azure) and mobile/embedded development (Flutter, ESP32), backed by 11 certifications rather than surface-level interest.
  • Evidence-based problem solving: I reconciled SIEM asset records against manufacturer/ground-truth data instead of trusting the dashboard, and built a statistical evaluation pipeline for MeetNote instead of assuming a plausible summary was a correct one.
  • Comfortable communicating up: I presented Genie's progress directly to the University's Vice President and drafted client-facing audit observations that needed to survive scrutiny from senior auditors.
W

Weaknesses

  • Breadth over depth: MeetNote, PulseSense and Genie are all still prototype or research-scale systems, not production-hardened ones.
  • My audit exposure was supervised, inside Protiviti's existing frameworks (SAMA CSF, ALR, RCM) — I have tested controls, not yet independently designed a compliance programme.
  • Documentation has occasionally lagged feature-building under deadline pressure, which is why MeetNote's evaluation methodology had to be strengthened retroactively before I trusted its results.
O

Opportunities

  • Converting supervised audit exposure into independently held expertise through a certification such as CISA, building directly on the Protiviti engagement.
  • Regional demand across Malaysia, Saudi Arabia and Egypt for graduates who combine applied AI with cybersecurity/audit literacy — an intersection I already occupy.
  • Taking one current prototype (most likely MeetNote) to a genuinely production-grade standard as a portfolio centrepiece.
T

Threats

  • Both fields I'm strongest in move fast — the specific LLM tooling I used will be superseded, and the threat landscape behind every SIEM finding keeps shifting.
  • Competition from Computer Science/Engineering peers who specialised earlier and narrower than I have.
  • Professional registration and certification pathways (CISA, chartered engineer status) require deliberate multi-year planning across jurisdictions, not something a degree provides automatically.

My strengths and opportunities point the same direction — cross-domain versatility backed by evidence — while my weaknesses and threats both come down to depth and independence, not capability. That is what tells me where to spend the next two years, and it is the thread running through my Programme Outcome evaluation below.

Engineering Knowledge & Problem Analysis PO1 · PO2

Grounded in certifications I sought independently to lock in fundamentals — CCNA and Network+ for networking, ITIL 4 for service management, Azure AI Fundamentals for applied AI — and demonstrated by reconciling SIEM asset records at Protiviti and by building a multi-metric statistical evaluation (TF-IDF, TextRank, ROUGE, BERTScore, significance testing) for MeetNote rather than trusting a plausible output. A full score here means the fundamentals hold; it does not mean they stop needing to be re-tested as tools change.

Design, Investigation & Modern Tools PO3 · PO4 · PO5

Evidenced by three delivered systems — Genie's UI/UX and Flutter feature set, MeetNote's privacy-first local AI pipeline, and PulseSense's Flutter frontend integrated with an ESP32 sensor glove — using Whisper, Ollama, FastAPI, LogRhythm, AWS and Azure. All three remain portfolio-scale rather than production-hardened, which is the honest gap behind this score.

Society, Sustainability & Ethics PO6 · PO7 · PO8

Genie was built to help students navigate university services directly; MeetNote's fully local architecture was a deliberate choice to avoid unnecessary cloud inference and the data-privacy and energy cost that comes with it; and my internship placed me inside a regulated compliance environment (SAMA CSF, IAM controls, audit evidence standards) that made ethical practice procedural rather than aspirational. Standards differ across the jurisdictions I've worked in, so this is a score I keep re-checking, not one I consider settled.

Teamwork, Communication & Project Management PO9 · PO10 · PO11

Built through the PulseSense five-person team, mentoring junior technical assistants, presenting Genie to the University's Vice President, drafting client-facing audit findings, and running Audit List of Requirements trackers and Risk & Control Matrices across concurrent Protiviti engagements. Professional-scale projects will be larger and higher-stakes than any of this, so scaling these skills up is an active goal, not a finished one.

Independent & Life-Long Learning PO12

The outcome I can evidence most concretely: eleven certifications across networking, cloud, cybersecurity and AI, almost entirely self-driven alongside my degree and internship. I read this score as describing a habit to sustain — most immediately, toward CISA and deeper cloud/security certifications — not a milestone already closed.

Full attainment is a baseline for professional practice, not a ceiling.

Overall Evaluation & Improvement

Reaching 1.00 across all twelve outcomes is a genuine foundation, but three concrete steps define what comes next for me: taking at least one prototype-scale system, most likely MeetNote, to a production-grade standard; pursuing a globally recognised certification such as CISA that converts my supervised audit experience into independently held expertise; and deliberately confirming how my qualifications transfer across the different regulatory environments I've already worked in, rather than assuming they do.

04 CONCLUSION

Ready, with a clear list of what's next.

This reflection has made one thing clearer than any transcript could: my strongest asset is not any single technical skill, it's the habit of verifying things before I trust them, carried from IT audit into AI engineering and back again. That habit produced eleven certifications, three delivered projects, and a five-month internship inside a regulated compliance environment, and it is what let me move between campus IT support, hardware assistance, AI development and formal audit work in under two years.

My Programme Outcome evaluation shows full attainment across all twelve outcomes, but this portfolio has been an exercise in reading that as a floor rather than a finish line: depth in one specialism, independent rather than supervised responsibility, and cross-jurisdiction credential planning are the concrete gaps my SWOT analysis surfaced, and they are what I intend to close first.

I am entering the engineering and technology profession with a solid ethical grounding from formal audit practice, real exposure to emerging AI and cloud tooling, and a demonstrated, self-directed pattern of learning. What I am not yet is finished — and I would rather be judged on that ongoing list than on a score that suggests I already am.
